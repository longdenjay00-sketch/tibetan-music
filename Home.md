# Tibetan music

Tibetan music is several practices that share a language, a plateau, and a Buddhist civilization. A monastery orchestra, a Lhasa dance song, an Amdo lute ballad, and an opera drum pattern are not one scale with different clothes.

Two sound worlds recur. Ritual sound is drone, cymbal cycle, and chant, led by a chant master. Secular song is voice plus lute, fiddle, and flute, much of it pentatonic. They meet in festivals, in [[Ache Lhamo]], and in the life of a town.

## Words used in this vault

| Word | Meaning here |
| --- | --- |
| Do | Scale degree 1. The flute often sets it. It is not a promise of the key of C or D. |
| Mode | Which degree of a collection feels like home. The notes can stay put while the home note moves. |
| 'don, rta, dbyangs | Three monastic melody types: recitation, discrete-pitch tune, contour chant. |
| Rol mo | Cymbals, and the rhythmic system they carry. Also a broad word for music. |
| Umdzé | dbu mdzad. The chant leader who holds the repertoire. |
| Yang-yig | dbyangs yig. Curves written over syllables. A contour sketch, not a staff. |
| Re-entrant | A lute tuning whose middle course is the highest, so the pitch order is not left to right. |
| Lineage | A monastery, college, or opera troupe with its own melodies. Names are not interchangeable. |
