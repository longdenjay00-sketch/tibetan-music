# Language, meter, and transmission

Tibetan music is oral first. Writing helps memory. It does not contain the piece the way a score contains a symphony. If you remember one thing from this vault, remember that.

## Language

Song and chant use more than one register.

- Liturgical chant may be Tibetan, Sanskrit mantra, or a Tibetan transposition of a Sanskrit text. Gyuto services include Sanskrit tantra titles. Pronunciation follows Tibetan habit, not a classroom in Benares.
- Literary song uses classical diction. Nangma poetry is the clear case.
- Folk song uses the dialect of its region. An Amdo dunglen lyric and a Lhasa toeshey lyric are not one language wearing two tunes.

Wylie transliteration in this vault is a finding aid. Pronunciations in [[Glossary]] are Central Tibetan approximations. Kham and Amdo will say the same spelling differently. The file names use the English spellings a listener actually searches: dranyen, dungchen, gyaling, nangma.

## Meter

In the treatise tradition, rhythm depends on the verse. You do not fit a poem into a pre-existing 4/4 grid. You sing the line, and the line's syllables and pauses are the time. Ellingson's description of 'don matches this: the pitch pattern and the rhythm pattern repeat, and they flex every time the words change.

Dance-songs are the exception that proves the social rule. The feet need a recurring pulse, so toeshey and circle dances hold a steady beat. Even there, the verse line is what fills the cycle. This vault does not assign those dances a Western time signature. Sources that print "4/4 or 6/8" are translating a dance pulse into a notation the source's readers already know.

Rol mo is the other system: counted beats, independent of a lyric, organized by rule. It belongs to instruments. See [[Rhythm and rol mo]]. A ritual can put text-rhythm and cymbal-counts in the same hour without confusing them. They are different clocks.

## How a piece is learned

An umdzé teaches chant by singing it, correcting it, and requiring it. Yang-yig curves and syllable signs, described in [[Yang notation]], remind a singer who already knows the contour. They do not teach a beginner the pitch. Gongche notation in the nangma community was a specialist tool of the same kind. It went out of ordinary use after the 1950s.

The dranyen is fretless. Finger placement is learned from a hand, not from a diagram. [[Dranyen]] gives the relative tuning and one published fingering map for the pentatonic set. It is a map. A teacher is the instrument.

Opera drum patterns are a closed vocabulary. The NTLA research found that a company may teach with several instructors at once, and that a play can be mounted in a few intensive rehearsals because the patterns, tunes, and role-types are already shared. Oral does not mean vague. It means the detail lives in people.

## What to write down in this vault

When you add a note of your own, write region, genre, who led, and what the words were doing. A melody transcription without those four is a fragment. The template in `templates/Note` is a place to start. Tag it `tibetan-music` so the graph keeps it.
