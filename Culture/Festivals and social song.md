# Festivals and social song

Most Tibetan music a person meets in life is occasional. A tune belongs to a season, a picnic, a circle, or a joke. The professional genres are a narrowing of that habit, not the other way around.

## Days that carry music

Losar, the new year, is the widest social festival: visiting, dress, dance, and song. Local calendars add monastery festivals, harvest gatherings, and horse-race or picnic days. Shoton, the yogurt festival at the end of summer, is the one English sources tie directly to opera. Historically the companies came to the Norbulingka. See [[Ache Lhamo]].

Summer picnics in the parks around Lhasa, including the gardens near the Norbulingka, were a standard setting for nangma players. TIPA describes hosts inviting dancers and musicians for those gatherings. The song texts often use Buddhist images even when the event is a party. Moral verse and love verse share the same melodic craft. The Sixth Dalai Lama's poems are the famous case of literary verse that performance kept alive.

## Kinds of social song

| Name | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Glu (lu) | Folk song. Often unaccompanied. Writers remark on a high, pulsed vocal timbre in some central styles. |
| Gzhas (zhe, shay) | Dance-song. The step and the verse are one piece. |
| La gzhas | Amdo love songs. |
| Nangma and stod gzhas | The Lhasa professional versions. [[Nangma and toeshey]]. |
| Street songs | Urban verses, often political or mocking, set to tunes people already know. |

Goldstein's study of Lhasa street songs describes a bardic habit in a place without newspapers. A common lyric frame is four lines of six syllables. Because the frame is stable, a new verse can ride an old melody. That is a compositional fact, not a curiosity. It is why satire spreads faster than new tunes.

Circle dances in Kham and group dances at Amdo gatherings use the same principle. The refrain is common property. The verse can be local, flirtatious, or topical.

## What the words tend to do

Texts return to landscape, horses, parents, lovers, religious advice, and praise of a place or a teacher. Epic performance is the long form of storytelling and has its own note, [[Gesar and storytelling]]. Opera aria is the theatrical form.

Six-syllable lines are common enough to affect melody. Short phrases, repeated pitches, and a cadence that waits for the line-end are what you hear when meter is in charge. [[Language meter and transmission]] is the theory side of that observation. Sakya Pandita already treated rhythm as something the poem imposes.
