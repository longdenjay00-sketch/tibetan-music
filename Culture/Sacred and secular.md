# Sacred and secular

Tibetan listeners already know which room a sound belongs to. A long horn on a roof is not a dance tune. A dranyen at a picnic is not a protector rite. The two rooms share players, festivals, and some words, and they stay distinct in function.

## What ritual sound is for

In a Buddhist ritual the sound is part of the offering and part of the timing. Cymbals mark sections. Drums cue the assembly. The conch calls. The chant is the text of the rite, sung in one of the three melodic types in [[Vocal styles]]. The leader is the umdzé. Mistakes are ritual mistakes, which is why Sakya Pandita's third chapter spends itself on attention, posture, and faults.

A masked dance, [[Cham]], is still this room. The public watches. The performers are ritual specialists, and the orchestra is the monastic one.

Chöd (gcod) sits in the same religious world and uses a small, portable set: damaru, kangling, and bell. It is associated with the tradition of Machik Labdrön (1055–1149). It is a meditation rite with sound, not a concert genre. Dorje and Ellingson have written on the symbolism of the chöd damaru. The short version is enough here. The hourglass drum and the thighbone trumpet are worked instruments and, at the same time, ritual objects. See [[Drums cymbals bell and damaru]].

## What secular sound is for

Secular song marks work, courtship, travel, satire, and hospitality. [[Festivals and social song]] is the social map. [[Nangma and toeshey]] is the urban professional version. [[Folk song and dunglen]] is the wider regional version. [[Ache Lhamo]] is theatre: story, aria, joke, and a drummer who directs the stage. Tibetans argue, as other people do, about whether a given opera or gar dance is religious. The practical test is the band and the occasion. Drum and cymbals at Shoton are theatre. The same shapes of drum and cymbal inside a protector rite are liturgy.

## Where music sits among the sciences

Classical Tibetan schooling inherited an Indian list.

The five major sciences (rig gnas che ba lnga) are grammar, logic, medicine, crafts and arts, and inner Buddhist knowledge. Music is treated inside the arts. Canzio's introduction to the treatise puts liturgical music there.

The five minor sciences (rig gnas chung ba lnga) are poetics, prosody, synonymics, drama, and astrology. Song sits against poetry and meter. Dance and opera sit against drama (zlos gar). TIPA describes music and dance as part of this minor set, which matches how an institute of performing arts actually teaches. It does not add an eleventh science called scales.

Astrology decides calendars, and calendars decide which liturgy is performed. That is the real join between skar rtsis and music. There is no traditional system in which a planet selects a pentatonic mode.

## Status

Ritual musicians inside a monastery have a rank, because the umdzé has a rank. Secular performers have not always been honored. Accounts of rebuilding lhamo in exile mention the old prejudice against actors and musicians, and the practical problem of getting families to allow children to train. The prejudice and the devotion to the art are both part of the record. Goldstein's work on Lhasa street songs shows the other edge: anonymous verses could say what a person could not sign.

## A working rule for this vault

If the sound supports a text the clergy holds, start in [[Buddhism and ritual sound]]. If the sound supports a lyric a layperson can sing at a gathering, start in [[Scales and pentatonic modes]]. If you cannot tell, name the instruments. The [[Instruments cheatsheet]] is the sorter.
